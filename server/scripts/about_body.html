About Ubufakazi

Ubufakazi means evidence

In isiZulu, ubufakazi means evidence or testimony. Ubufakazi is a digital-forensics tool for scam victims and banks: it turns victims' evidence into tamper-evident case files and links them across bank branches, so the bank sees the syndicate, freezes the mule account and escalates to SAPS.

The problem

One syndicate, many branches

A syndicate scams victims in many provinces. Each victim's report lands as a small fraud case at their own branch, so no single branch sees the network, and the mule account stays open.

What we do

Evidence that holds up

Every file is fingerprinted with SHA-256 the moment it arrives and kept read-only. Facts are extracted, a timeline built, and a draft statement written in the victim's language.

Why it matters

Ten victims is a syndicate case

When reports at different branches share a mule account, phone number, website or message script, the syndicate board shows the branches involved, a suggested lead branch and the bank's decision.

Who it serves and who pays

Bank branches

Fraud investigators

Structured, tamper-evident cases linked to the same scammer across branches, even when phone numbers change. The bank escalates to SAPS with a sealed case pack.

Banks · paying customers

Fraud teams

Banks and fintechs subscribe to syndicate alerts and a fraud-report API: early warning when many victims paid the same mule account, while it can still be frozen.

Funding the free service

Insurers, grants, CSI

Insurers can licence data for scam claims. Grants, CSI and sponsors fund the free victim reporting that feeds the evidence.

Free for victims

Victims report at no cost. The banks that save money by freezing mule accounts earlier pay for the service, and SAPS receives stronger, linked cases from the bank.

How we start

A pilot with one bank and its branches in one province: measure how many mule accounts are flagged and frozen, then agree subscription pricing per institution by alert volume.

Branch routing

Every report goes to the fraud desk of the bank branch whose region covers the town where the victim was scammed (each province has three or four branches, each covering its own towns). If the town is unknown, the province's default branch receives it. The bank (Ubuntu Trust Bank), its branches and their regions are fictional; a real deployment would use the partner bank's own branch network.

How linking works

Signals we compare

  • Receiving bank account, phone number, email, website domain, social profile, @username, payment reference: compared by a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256), so raw values never leave the server
  • Message template: if 35% or more of the three-word phrases match (at least 6 phrases)

How we label them

  • Single lead: one kind of shared signal
  • Corroborated lead: two or more different kinds
  • Every lead shows its reason and any conflicts. It is a reason to investigate, never proof: accounts can belong to money mules and numbers can be spoofed

Privacy and safety

Branch-isolated

Investigators see only their own branch's reports. Other branches' reports appear as "restricted", with no details.

No ID, no GPS

We never ask for ID numbers. Location is optional and rounded to about 1 km. Banks receive account-level alerts only, never victims' names or statements.

Audited

Every view, download, verification and status change is written to a hash-chained audit log, so changes to history are detectable.

What Ubufakazi is not

  • Not SAPS, and not connected to SAPS or any real bank. No CAS numbers are issued: when the bank escalates to SAPS, its investigator enters the CAS number SAPS issued, and it is not checked with SAPS.
  • Not proof of guilt. Automatic extraction can miss or misread details; investigators must check every fact against the original files.
  • Not tamper-proof. It makes changes detectable: SHA-256 fingerprints plus independent RFC 3161 timestamps from FreeTSA.
  • A demonstration: the bank (Ubuntu Trust Bank), its branches and earlier reports are fictional, synthetic data.
