About Ubufakazi

Ubufakazi means evidence

In isiZulu, ubufakazi means evidence or testimony. Ubufakazi is a digital-forensics tool for SAPS and banks: it turns scam victims' evidence into tamper-evident case files and links them across station jurisdictions, so investigators see the syndicate and banks can freeze the mule account.

The problem

One syndicate, many dockets

A syndicate scams victims in many provinces. Each victim opens a small docket at their own station, so no single station sees the network, and the mule account stays open.

What we do

Evidence that holds up

Every file is fingerprinted with SHA-256 the moment it arrives and kept read-only. Facts are extracted, a timeline built, and a draft statement written in the victim's language.

Why it matters

Ten victims is a syndicate case

When reports in different jurisdictions share a mule account, phone number, website or message script, the syndicate board shows the stations involved, a suggested lead station and the bank's decision.

Who it serves and who pays

SAPS

Investigators

Structured, tamper-evident cases linked to the same scammer across station jurisdictions, even when phone numbers change.

Banks · paying customers

Fraud teams

Banks and fintechs subscribe to syndicate alerts and a fraud-report API: early warning when many victims paid the same mule account, while it can still be frozen.

Funding the free service

Insurers, grants, CSI

Insurers can licence data for scam claims. Grants, CSI and sponsors fund the free victim reporting that feeds the evidence.

Free for victims and SAPS

Victims report at no cost, and SAPS stations use the console and syndicate board at no cost. The banks that save money by freezing mule accounts earlier pay for the service.

How we start

A pilot with one bank and the stations in one province: measure how many mule accounts are flagged and frozen, then agree subscription pricing per institution by alert volume.

Jurisdiction routing

Every report goes to the demo station whose jurisdiction covers the town where the victim was scammed (each province has three or four demo stations, each covering its own towns). If the town is unknown, the province's default station receives it. The demo stations and their jurisdictions are fictional, not official SAPS precinct boundaries; a real deployment would use SAPS's own boundaries.

How linking works

Signals we compare

  • Receiving bank account, phone number, email, website domain, social profile, @username, payment reference: compared by a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256), so raw values never leave the server
  • Message template: if 35% or more of the three-word phrases match (at least 6 phrases)

How we label them

  • Single lead: one kind of shared signal
  • Corroborated lead: two or more different kinds
  • Every lead shows its reason and any conflicts. It is a reason to investigate, never proof: accounts can belong to money mules and numbers can be spoofed

Privacy and safety

Station-isolated

Officers see only their own station's reports. Other stations' reports appear as "restricted", with no details.

No ID, no GPS

We never ask for ID numbers. Location is optional and rounded to about 1 km. Banks receive account-level alerts only, never victims' names or statements.

Audited

Every view, download, verification and status change is written to a hash-chained audit log, so changes to history are detectable.

What Ubufakazi is not

  • Not SAPS, and not connected to SAPS or any real bank. No CAS numbers are issued: an officer enters the CAS number when the case is registered, and it is not checked with SAPS.
  • Not proof of guilt. Automatic extraction can miss or misread details; officers must check every fact against the original files.
  • Not tamper-proof. It makes changes detectable: SHA-256 fingerprints plus independent RFC 3161 timestamps from FreeTSA.
  • A demonstration: stations, the bank (Ubuntu Trust Bank) and earlier reports are fictional, synthetic data.
